1. Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood

    Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood teaches children how to control and understand their emotions. It does not overstimulate them, instead, it calms them down. Inspired by Mister Rodger's Neighborhood, the show keeps his work alive by encouraging kids to be kind, imaginative, and understand how special they are.


  2. Sesame Street

    For over 56 years, Sesame Street has been educating children around the world through songs, humor, and adorable puppets. It is so well-rounded that even A-list celebrities make guest appearances. What I love most about Sesame Street is the positivity it spreads. The writers do not use sarcasm or crude humor; The scripts are tender for a tender audience.

  3. Bluey

    There is a reason Bluey took the world by storm. Centering an animated family of Australian heelers, Bluey explores real-life, down-to-earth topics. It emphasizes family, community, and self-discovery. Its art style is outstanding, and so is its writing. This show makes parents laugh and cry along with their children and reminds everyone to hold loved ones tighter.


  4. Curious George

    Children are curious from birth, so they relate to George, a curious monkey who gets in sticky situations and sometimes saves the day. George is beloved by children and helps them understand the importance of curiosity and problem-solving.


  5. Lego Friends

    Targeted for an older audience than the shows above, Lego Friends is colorful and adventurous. With friendship at its core, children are given good role models with outstanding leadership and a taste of suspense without fear. The main characters in this show are also changemakers who inspire children to make a difference in the world using their gifts.
